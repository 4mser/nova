Executive summary

An on-board doctor that doesn't wait for Earth.

On a trip to Mars, a consult with the flight surgeon takes 6 to 44 minutes round trip, and during solar conjunction the link drops for weeks. NOVA handles monitoring, detection and first response inside the ship. Vital signs flow through a core built on F Prime; the crew sees them on the screen, on their phone and over the patient's body in augmented reality; a local assistant answers from open medical sources. Earth receives a report every night and sends back signed updates.

Three principles

01 · All on board

No critical function depends on the cloud or on Earth. Models, library, voice and vision run on the same ship computer.

02 · Fixed rules

Thresholds live in the flight core and emergency steps come from a reviewable file. The model explains and assists; it does not decide.

03 · The person decides

Each crew member chooses what to share. Mood leaves the phone only if they share their day; what they write never leaves.

From the station to the screen in under a second

A real case from the demo: the simulator applies the “fainting” scenario and Ingrid's pulse drops to 34 beats per minute. This is how that value passes through each piece, top to bottom in time.

Reading · 1 Hz. One message per measurement with crew member, sensor, value, unit and quality from 0 to 1. Max 1 KB.

Classification. Plausible range per sensor; quality below 0.5 does not alert. Raising the level is immediate; lowering it takes 10 s in a row.

Silence · 120 s. If a sensor that has reported goes quiet, the core emits SIN_DATOS.

Status · 1 Hz. 4 crew members × 18 sensors with value, level and age; each alert goes out instantly.

Screens. NOVA PC gets the continuous stream; phones read /v1/estado every 5 s.

Earth. The report goes down with light delay; commands go up through a queue with a computed arrival time.

10  ·  Part I · Architecture  ·  Medical station

A data contract to connect any hardware

The medical station talks to the core through an open contract: one JSON line per measurement (NDJSON). If the message meets the contract, the core understands it without knowing which board is behind it. Luis's station, the simulator and the phones come in through the same door.

  Transport

UDP to port 50050, one message per datagram, max 1 KB, UTF-8 ending in a newline. The contract also proposes USB serial at 115,200 baud to avoid depending on the network; the Estacion component reads only UDP today. Phones come in through /v1/lote.

  One measurement

{"v":1,"tipo":"medicion","est":"estacion-1",
"t":1763164800123,"trip":"T2","sensor":"spo2",
"valor":97,"unidad":"%","calidad":0.92}

The unit is checked against the contract table; if it does not match, the message is dropped with its reason.

The simulator: real variability and five scenarios

Until the physical station is present, estacion/simulador/simulador.py sends messages with the same contract. Each warning comes from fixed rules, not the model: what changed, what to do and the source. Low pulse and pressure together open the fainting guide. Acknowledge mutes it 2 min if the level does not rise.

14  ·  Vitals and forecast

Each sign's history and what comes in the next 15 minutes

The Vitals view draws the history stored on board (1 Hz for 6 h and per minute for 30 days, on disk; windows of 10 min to 7 days) with the core's current thresholds on top, projects each sign forward and warns when something departs from what is expected.

Kenji's pulse, last hour. In lilac, the 15 min cone: 55 ±18 bpm, with a 14% chance of landing at the attention threshold (≤ 45).

Trend. Weighted least squares over that person's last 30 min, with more weight on recent data (15 min constant).

Cone. Projects a quarter of the chosen window, up to 15 min. Width is ±2 deviations and grows with the horizon.

Crossing probability. The chance of passing the attention threshold at the end of the horizon. Above 30% the cone turns amber.

Unexpected change. If the latest reading lands more than 3 deviations from what the trend expected, the panel marks it as unexpected change.

  Honest scope

Simple, explainable statistics on each person's data, in the spirit of world models: predict and act on the prediction. It is neither a learned model nor clinically validated. It runs only with readings from the last 30 s.

16  ·  Mind

How the crew is doing mentally, told with care

Isolation shows first in sleep and mood. The Mind view joins two sources: each person's live signs compared with their own 24 h average, and the daily check-in each crew member chooses to share from their phone.

Kenji, day 220. The brain shows “Stressed” from a mood of 2/5 and 6.0 h of sleep. On the right, 14 days of mood for all four; below, sleep, energy and body signals against each person's usual.

  Six states with fixed rules

Rest, focus, stress, fatigue, calm and high arousal. Physical cases first: pulse −12 and pressure −25 against the usual (low blood to the brain), SpO2 under 92 %, glucose under 70 or fever from 38 °C. Then a score from pulse, breathing and variability, and last the check-in: mood ≤ 2/5 or under 6 h of sleep. Each state shows its reasons in plain words.

  Private by default

Mood, energy and sleep reach the ship only if the person shares their day; what they write in their journal never leaves the phone. NOVA names them with care and only when they matter (rule 6 of its prompt). On Earth, the flight surgeon gets summaries with consent via /v1/privado.

  A model to explain

The particle brain comes from OpenPsico (B&N, three.js r170, served locally) and its activity profiles are conceptual: they are not fitted to EEG or MRI. It helps the crew understand what happens in the body; it does not measure the mind.

17  ·  Mind · Constellations

Each crew member's week, drawn as their sky

Constellations is a new tab in Mind. It shows mood, energy and sleep for the last 7 or 14 days of those who share their day from the phone. The ship does not invent days: it reads one journal per person and, if someone does not share, their sky stays empty and says so.

Each person. Four compact constellations, the same as in each app: height is mood, each star's size is hours slept and the card compares the week with the previous one. Demo data.

  Two readings

Each person (default) shows each one's constellation with its palette and a dot that runs through the week. Together puts the whole crew on one chart, with a cursor per day, buttons to show or hide people and a table view with a fixed name column. Colors are a categorical palette validated for dark backgrounds, fixed per seat.

  Only what the person shares

In the app, “Share my day” sends the ship the last 14 days of check-in and sleep, each with its date. The ship stores them in a journal per person (/v1/diario) with the name of who wrote it. The simulator no longer invents mood, energy and sleep for real people. The card warns if the check-in was the same every day.

  What it is for

Mood dropping in several people at once points to something in the environment (sleep, workload, an event) more than to one person. It lets the crew and the psychologist on Earth talk with data; it classifies no one.

25  ·  Part III · Onboard vision

The phone sees, the ship thinks

The scanner works like a headset tethered to a computer. The phone shows its own camera without waiting on the network and sends each frame to the ship, which runs the heavy models on its GPU and returns only the body points. If the ship does not answer, the phone does the vision with light models and says so on screen.

  No queue

Vision always processes the newest frame and drops old ones. An answer for frame N closes all earlier ones, and a frame with no answer in 400 ms is counted as lost. The hologram never falls behind by piling up work.

  The patient first

With two people in view, the patient is the one with the most horizontal torso: the one standing is the rescuer. Each phone has its own tracker (up to 4) so two cameras do not mix up the same person. The mask is cropped to the patient only.

  Self-calibrating gyroscope

Gyroscope axes differ between phones. NOVA learns them by comparing the measured rotation with how the body points moved, and only uses the sign when the relation is clear. It can be turned off from the station.

  Secure page on the ship network

The phone's browser only gives the camera to a secure page. The ship generates its certificate with rcgen (localhost, nova.local and its IP), stores it in ~/.nova-pc/tls and remakes it if the IP changes. The ship starts vision, restarts it if it crashes, and vision shuts down if the ship dies.

Where it is. Position comes from a least-squares fit of MediaPipe's 3D points against the same points in the image, with a perspective camera (67° field of view on the phone, 60° on a PC, adjustable from the station). This gives the real distance.

How big it is. Shoulder width (MediaPipe × 1.18, calibrated) and torso length are averaged over the first frames. The anatomy is scaled between 0.85 and 1.35 times the reference, and length does not change if the hips leave the frame.

27  ·  Body scan · Eyes

The eyes, measured with the camera and compared with oneself

Spaceflight-associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS) and tiredness show in the eyes. The scanner adds the Eyes system with Nicolás Richardson's ocular vision method: MediaPipe Face Landmarker on the same device, at full resolution, with the same model he measured (sha256 verified).

Eyes live. Opening of each eye, upper and lower eyelid to pupil, blinks per minute, distance, head stillness and alertness level. Tested on a team member at Space Apps, looking at the Eye check red dot.

  Measures and their noise

Millimeters assume an 11.7 mm iris. There is a stillness gate and blinks are excluded. The 20 s “Eye check” looking at a red dot stores the median on the ship (/v1/ojos) and compares it with that person's and that camera's baseline. It only asks for follow-up if the change exceeds the minimal detectable change (MDC95) Richardson measured: 1.8 mm in opening, 1.2 mm in upper eyelid and 0.9 mm in lower.

  Tiredness, not emotions

PERCLOS over the last minute (eyes ≥ 80 % closed; Dinges and Grace, 1998): from 7.5 % “some signs”, from 15 % “tired”. It adds blink duration, closures of 0.5 s or more and yawns. An Eye check with tiredness in the last 12 h shows up in Mind with its reason. NOVA knows it does not read emotions from the face.

  What it does not do

It does not diagnose SANS: it flags a change and suggests repeating the next day and reading a near-vision chart. Pupil–brow distance is not used because its MDC (3.3 mm) does not yet allow useful changes to be seen. It was measured with one camera and one person; clinical validation is pending.

29  ·  Part III · CPR by gravity

No weight, no compression: the technique changes with gravity

On Earth the rescuer's weight does the work. On Mars it weighs 38 % and in cruise nothing. NOVA picks the technique by mission phase (cruise = microgravity, Mars = 0.38 g) or the one the crew sets, marks the compression point on the chest and counts compressions by watching the hands.

Microgravity CPR mode, live at the hackathon. Compression point, 100–120 per minute and 5–6 cm, the Evetts-Russomano technique with its source and the reaction arrow for anchoring.

  Counting compressions

Only hands within a quarter of the body size from the CPR point are used. Their vertical motion, normalized by body size, marks each compression. The rate is the median of the intervals over the last 5 s. A compression is good if the rate is between 100 and 120, and its depth between 5 and 6 cm when the camera sees from the side.

  When to switch rescuers

NOVA compares four plans over a 3-minute horizon (switch now, in 40 s, at 2:00 or keep going) and suggests the one that loses the fewest quality minutes. It assumes exponential fatigue that sets in sooner in microgravity (140 s) than on Mars (170 s) or Earth (240 s), counts 5 s hands-off per switch and moves it up if the rate drops below 100.

  The floating patient

In microgravity a push moves the patient away. NOVA measures how far the body drifts in one second (compressions go back and forth and cancel out), subtracts the phone's rotation and, above 12 cm/s, draws its path and asks to hold the patient before compressing.

  A voice that corrects little

NOVA speaks when something changes: the technique for this gravity, a patient drifting away or a rate out of range (“Faster. Aim for 100 to 120 a minute.”). It leaves at least 4 s between sentences. The first counter was tuned with a real CPR video: it counted 8 of 11 compressions with no false positives.

The bridge detects each beat in the AD8232 wave, computes pulse, variability (RMSSD) and signal quality, and sends the wave to the ship every 0.2 s. The tablet and the PC draw it the same way; pulse and HRV go into that person’s record and into the F Prime core as real data.

Isolated part. A click on the heart board leaves it alone with its info card: what it is, sampling, bridge and what it sends.

  First who, then their sheet

Without a session the wave is not shown. With a card, the dock’s ECG button is marked “live” and measuring starts when the sheet opens. A loose electrode shows in amber with a flat line. Pulse and HRV are saved only with the sheet open and clean beats (quality ≥ 0.5). Without electrodes, values can be entered by hand under that person.

A networked board (ESP32) that talks straight to the ship with no PC in between, wave analysis inside the flight core (contract onda messages) and clinical-grade electrodes validated against a reference monitor.

  Adapted expected range

Personal baseline + a ridge corrector learned from other subjects + that person's first 28 days in bed. It is the strongest result: at the same false alarm rate, the weight range is cut in half.

  7-day forecast

PatchTST (Hugging Face transformers, trained on Kaggle) narrowly beats the 7-day mean. Whether pre-training on Earth data helps is still open. 27 tests pass.

  How it connects to the ship

It uses the names in the station contract (pulso, pa_sis, pa_dia, fr, temp, peso). It is designed as one more local service, on port 8793. The team decided not to propose it yet for ESPERADO_VUELO: the fixed emergency thresholds do not change.

  Stated limits

Bed rest reproduces the fluid shift and body unloading, not radiation, CO2 or isolation. It is not validated with real flight. Run v4 (pulse and pressure, 3 seeds, per-person adaptation) is decided and not yet run. 65-page LaTeX report with 91 references.
