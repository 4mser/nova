Learn each person's normal on Earth and adjust it in flight

Benjamín Campos tested whether NOVA can learn each crew member's baseline on Earth and adapt to their “new normal” with the first days of flight. Since there are no public daily series of in-flight vital signs, he used the most studied ground analog: NASA's −6° head-down bed rest.

  Adapted expected range

Personal baseline + a ridge corrector learned from other subjects + that person's first 28 days in bed. It is the strongest result: at the same false alarm rate, the weight range is cut in half.

  7-day forecast

PatchTST (Hugging Face transformers, trained on Kaggle) narrowly beats the 7-day mean. Whether pre-training on Earth data helps is still open. 27 tests pass.

  How it connects to the ship

It uses the names in the station contract (pulso, pa_sis, pa_dia, fr, temp, peso). It is designed as one more local service, on port 8793. The team decided not to propose it yet for ESPERADO_VUELO: the fixed emergency thresholds do not change.

  Stated limits

Bed rest reproduces the fluid shift and body unloading, not radiation, CO₂ or isolation. It is not validated with real flight. Run v4 (pulse and pressure, 3 seeds, per-person adaptation) is decided and not yet run. 65-page LaTeX report with 91 references.
