How the crew is doing mentally, told with care

Isolation shows first in sleep and mood. The Mind view joins two sources: each person's live signs compared with their own 24 h average, and the daily check-in each crew member chooses to share from their phone.

Kenji, day 220. The brain shows “Stressed” from a mood of 2/5 and 6.0 h of sleep. On the right, 14 days of mood for all four; below, sleep, energy and body signals against each person's usual.

  Six states with fixed rules

Rest, focus, stress, fatigue, calm and high arousal. Physical cases first: pulse −12 and pressure −25 against the usual (low blood to the brain), SpO₂ under 92 %, glucose under 70 or fever from 38 °C. Then a score from pulse, breathing and variability, and last the check-in: mood ≤ 2/5 or under 6 h of sleep. Each state shows its reasons in plain words.

  Private by default

Mood, energy and sleep reach the ship only if the person shares their day; what they write in their journal never leaves the phone. NOVA names them with care and only when they matter (rule 6 of its prompt). On Earth, the flight surgeon gets summaries with consent via /v1/privado.

  A model to explain

The particle brain comes from OpenPsico (B&N, three.js r170, served locally) and its activity profiles are conceptual: they are not fitted to EEG or MRI. It helps the crew understand what happens in the body; it does not measure the mind.
