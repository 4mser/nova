Women’s health, by design

Most flight data still comes from men. NOVA follows what NASA has published and keeps the most private data on each astronaut’s phone: nothing about the cycle reaches the crew screen.

TopicWhat NASA knowsWhat the app does
ClotsIn about half the crew, blood slows in the jugular. Today everyone gets an ultrasound.Logs the ultrasound, flags estrogen methods; neck, leg or chest are urgent.
MenstruationMany astronauts suppress it for hygiene and waste; station water is recycled from urine.Private tracking or suppression, daily pill and containment kit on board.
SuppliesThree years is about 1,100 pills, with no stability tests in deep-space radiation.Counts pills and pads against the mission days left.
Anemia and urineIrregular bleeding can lead to anemia; urinary health has its own report.Warns on heavy bleeding with low energy, or burning when urinating.
ReturnDizziness on standing after flight is more common in women.30 days before landing: stand test, fluids and compression.
RadiationA 600 mSv career limit, the same for everyone.Shows the dose against 600 mSv; today with a sample value.

Exercise is part of the treatment

The body twin in NOVA PC projects each crew member with and without their exercise plan. It uses NASA’s rates: bone loses 1 to 1.5 % per month without exercise and resistance exercise cuts that in half; muscle, up to 20 % without countermeasures.
