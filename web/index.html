NOVA is a working prototype built for NASA Space Apps 2026. It runs on a ship computer around NASA's F Prime flight software, with every model on board. The crew and their numbers are demo data.

How it works

Everything runs on board.

No internet and no cloud during the mission. A medical station and each astronaut's phone feed NASA's F Prime flight core, and five small models on the ship's own computer turn those numbers into care the crew can act on.

Exercise

Exercise is part of the treatment.

Without weight on them, the hip and leg bones lose 1 to 1.5% of their density each month, and posture muscles shrink fast. NOVA's body twin projects every crew member with and without their exercise plan, so skipping a session has a visible cost.

Mara Reyes, day 219 of a demo mission. Rates from the NASA Human Research Program (bone fracture and muscle evidence reports) and NASA OCHMO-MTB-013 (2026): resistance exercise on the ARED protects bone about equally in women and men (Smith et al., JBMR 2014).

Women's health

Women's health, built in.

About half of NASA's new astronaut candidates are women, yet most spaceflight data still comes from men. NOVA follows what NASA has learned so far, and keeps the most private data on her own phone.

  1. Cycle care that stays private

    Track the cycle or suppress it with a continuous pill, implant or hormonal IUD. Nothing reaches the crew screen; only a summary goes to the flight surgeon, and only if she chooses.

    Jain & Wotring, npj Microgravity 2016 · npj Microgravity 2025
  2. Clots in the neck veins

    In weightlessness, blood slows in the jugular vein in about half of crew members, women and men. A clot was found on an astronaut taking a combined pill. NASA now screens everyone by ultrasound; NOVA reminds the scan, marks methods with estrogen and treats a swollen neck, leg or chest pain as urgent.

    Marshall-Goebel et al., JAMA Netw Open 2019 · NASA OCHMO-MTB-007, 2026
  3. Supplies until home

    Pills and pads counted against the days left in the mission. For a three-year mission that is about 1,100 pills.

    Jain & Wotring, npj Microgravity 2016
  4. Anemia and urinary health

    Heavy bleeding with low energy, or burning when peeing, points her to the flight surgeon and the right test in the kit.

    Frontiers in Physiology 2025 · NASA OCHMO-MTB-003
  5. Ready to stand up again

    Dizziness on standing after landing is more common in women. Before landing, NOVA plans a stand test, fluids and compression garments.

    npj Microgravity 2025
  6. One radiation limit

    NASA now uses 600 mSv for everyone, set for the most sensitive case: a woman early in her career. NOVA counts every dose against it.

    NASA-STD-3001 · OCHMO-TB-020

NOVA is not a medical device: care signals point to the flight surgeon and are not a diagnosis. Reproductive health in long missions is still an open research question (Nature, npj Women's Health 2024).

Every night

While the crew sleeps,
Nova goes home.

A report of about 6 KB leaves the F Prime core as CCSDS packets and crosses up to 22 light-minutes to Earth. Updates come back signed with ed25519, and the ship checks the signature before installing them and rolls back if anything fails. The next stage is knowledge distillation: a larger teacher model on Earth learns from each report, flight surgeons review every change, and it trains the smaller model that flies on board.

FAQ

Ask before launch.

Is NOVA real?

Yes. NOVA is a working prototype our team built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, for the challenge "Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions". The flight core, the ship screens, the station tablet, the phone app and the body scan all run today. The station measures ECG today; the other sensors are the next stage. The crew and their numbers are demo data.

Does it need a connection to Earth?

No. Every model runs on the ship's computer, offline. Once a night, NOVA sends Mission Control a report of about 6 KB, and improvements come back signed, with the real delay of light.

Who sees what an astronaut shares?

She or he decides. Vital signs go to the flight surgeon. Mood leaves the phone only if they choose to share their day, cycle care stays on the phone unless she sends a summary, and the journal never leaves it.

How does NOVA handle women's health?

It follows what NASA has learned: private cycle care or menstrual suppression, the neck-vein ultrasound NASA now requires, urgent signs for clots, supplies counted to the end of the mission, anemia and urinary checks, and readiness for landing.

Does Nova replace the flight surgeon?

No. Emergency steps come from a reviewed file with their source and page, and the limits live in the flight core. Nova explains and guides; the crew and their doctors decide.

Where does the knowledge come from?

41 open medical sources on board (35,830 passages), NASA's Human Research Program evidence, NASA-STD-3001, open spaceflight data from OSDR and public heart and sleep datasets from PhysioNet.
